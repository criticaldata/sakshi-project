Join the auditor network

SAKSHI will be run by trained community auditors in the places where health AI is deployed. The network is now being formed.

Who auditors are

Auditor teams will include:

  • medical students and clinical trainees;
  • clinicians, including clinical officers and nurses;
  • patient advocates and members of civil society organizations;
  • data scientists and biomedical engineers;
  • public health practitioners.

Clinical and data-science expertise will support reviews of evidence and technical performance. Auditors’ knowledge of local health systems will inform reviews of regulatory pathways and financial relationships. Members of affected communities will contribute their understanding of patients’ experiences.

Local auditors will conduct the reviews and assess the evidence under the protocol. They will author the findings.

Regional hubs

Regional community hubs will conduct reviews locally. They will draw on the MIT Critical Data consortium's presence in over 40 countries, a network built through more than a hundred datathons over the past decade.

Each hub will train local auditors and maintain a record of those trained to use the protocol. Teams will periodically compare their assessments with profiles from other hubs. The shared protocol will allow communities to present locally gathered evidence to the institutions responsible for deployment decisions.

Auditor training

Training will include a self-directed online module and a workshop using a standardized reference case, followed by a supervised contribution. Auditors will refresh their training as reference cases change and disclose relevant interests before contributing to a profile. See Governance.

Partnerships

SAKSHI is seeking partners who could use published findings in reporting, advocacy, and decisions about health AI:

  • Investigative journalists, who investigate and report on the findings.
  • Health law scholars, who contribute expertise in regulatory interpretation and responsible reporting.
  • Medical specialty societies, whose members encounter these systems in practice and can judge clinical claims.
  • Patient advocacy organizations, who contribute patients’ perspectives on transparency, informed consent, and the use of AI in care.
  • Regulators and procurement bodies, who cite profiles in procurement decisions and draw on documented patterns when drafting future guidance.
