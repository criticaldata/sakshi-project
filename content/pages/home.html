Community review of clinical AI evidence.

SAKSHI will train local auditors to assess the evidence for health AI in the settings where it is used. The research project is in development at MIT Critical Data.

Status. SAKSHI is a proposal under development. No profiles have been published, and the framework’s claims have not yet been tested.

01 / What is SAKSHI

MIT Critical Data

Local review of clinical AI.

In Sanskrit, sākṣī means witness. SAKSHI will support local auditors reviewing the evidence for clinical AI in their communities.

Trained local auditors will use a standardized four-domain protocol. Their findings will form a public evidence record for clinicians, researchers, health services, patient advocates, and regulators.

Reviews will document the evidence supporting clinical claims, its limitations, and questions that require further study.

01Evidence-based review

Auditors will assess publications, public filings, and interviews. Companies will be invited to provide evidence and respond to findings.

02Led by local auditors

Local auditors will identify relevant questions and assess the evidence under the protocol. They will write the findings.

03A shared protocol

Every regional hub will use the same protocol, allowing readers to compare findings across settings.

04Updated as systems change

Profiles will be updated when the deployed system changes.

The gap

Why review deployed systems?

  • Regulatory clearance concerns market entry.

    Clearance establishes a basis for market entry. It does not establish how a system performs after deployment or in another country.

  • Independent evaluators often lack access.

    Technical audits examine a model at a fixed moment and often require access to its internals. Independent evaluators rarely have that access. Such audits also provide limited evidence about whether a company’s public claims are justified.

  • Local evidence can be difficult to find.

    Published studies and public records may provide limited information about performance in a particular community. Local review will help identify what is known and what still needs evaluation.

02 / The Protocol

The four-domain protocol

Each profile will examine the evidence behind a system’s claims and its route to market. It will also document financial incentives and patients’ experiences.

01

Clinical evidence audit

Published claims against the actual evidence base.

  • Are there registered trials?
  • Is validation peer-reviewed?
  • Were study populations representative of the deployment context?
02

Regulatory pathway analysis

How the product entered the market and what oversight it is subject to.

  • Sandbox exemption or clearance?
  • Does clearance from one jurisdiction imply validation in another?
  • Is post-market surveillance in place?
03

Financial disclosure map

Investor and board relationships; incentive structures.

  • How might financial arrangements influence clinical use or evaluation?
  • Could government or institutional relationships affect independent evaluation?
04

Patient experience reporting

Informed consent, workflow integration, recourse mechanisms.

  • Do patients know AI is involved in their care?
  • What happens when the clinician disagrees with the output?
  • How can a patient seek recourse?

Each domain will describe the available evidence and explain gaps relevant to the deployment. The profile will include the company’s reply and a correction log, without a single numerical score. A separate technical evaluation will assess calibration, subgroup fairness, robustness, and dataset shift.

03 / Contributors

The people developing SAKSHI.

SAKSHI’s contributors work in nine countries, including several regions where the platform will operate. They include clinicians, clinical trainees, public-health and informatics researchers, and data scientists. MIT Critical Data coordinates the work from Cambridge, Massachusetts.

  • United States

Editorial board

An independent editorial group is planned to review profiles before publication. Its membership and conflict of interest disclosures will be published once it is established. See Governance.

Auditors

Community auditors will complete training in evidence appraisal, research ethics, and the review protocol. Their contributions will be reviewed through the project’s training and editorial process before publication.

Contact

Join the auditor network.

SAKSHI is forming its first regional hubs. Medical students, clinicians, patient advocates, data scientists, and public health practitioners in settings where health AI is deployed are invited to train as auditors. Partnerships with investigative journalists, health law scholars, medical specialty societies, patient advocacy organizations, and regulators are also sought.
