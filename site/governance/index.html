Status. SAKSHI is a proposal under development. No profiles have been published, and the framework’s claims have not yet been tested.

Governance

Governance and independence

SAKSHI proposes requirements for auditor training, conflicts of interest, independent review, and corrections.

How reviews will work

Local teams will use a shared protocol to assess evidence in their own clinical and community settings.

  • Evidence-based review

    Auditors will use publications, public filings, and interviews. Companies will be invited to supply relevant evidence and respond to findings. A lack of response will be recorded without being treated as evidence of a problem.

  • Led by local auditors

    Local auditors will identify relevant questions and assess the evidence under the protocol. Their knowledge of clinical practice and community needs will inform the review.

  • A shared protocol

    Every regional hub will use the same protocol and document the local context so readers can compare findings across settings.

  • Updated as evidence changes

    Profiles will be updated when new studies, product changes, or verified corrections affect the findings.

Auditor training

Auditors will complete the following training before working independently on a profile.

  1. Self-directed online module

    Covers the four review domains, evidence appraisal, research ethics, source documentation, and responsible reporting.

  2. Live workshop

    Trainees apply the protocol to a standardized reference case and receive structured feedback on it.

  3. Supervised field contribution

    Trainees will contribute to a profile under a senior auditor before working independently.

Auditors will refresh their training against updated reference cases. Hub coordinators will periodically compare assessments from different teams to support consistent use of the protocol.

A Delphi process among hub coordinators and senior auditors is the planned mechanism for setting the shared thresholds that calibration is checked against: what counts as adequate validation evidence, and what counts as a disqualifying conflict. That process is still under way, and its output will be validated before it is treated as settled. See the evaluation plan.

Training and readiness assessments will be specific to participation in SAKSHI research.

Conflict of interest disclosure

Before participating in any profile, auditors complete a disclosure covering three kinds of relationship:

  • financial relationships with the company under review or its direct competitors;
  • advisory or employment relationships with health ministries or procurement bodies involved in the deployment;
  • membership in civil society organizations receiving funding from health AI companies in the same therapeutic or geographic space.

The requirements follow the disclosure model used in academic peer review and equity research. Auditors with material conflicts are recused. Relationships below the recusal threshold are noted in the published profile.

Disclosures will help the planned editorial group and readers assess whether a relationship may have influenced a finding. The group will review disclosed relationships alongside the evidence supporting the finding.

Independent review

The proposed process will exclude commercial sponsorship of individual profiles by the company under review. Reviewers with material conflicts will not participate in the associated review.

Funding and institutional relationships relevant to a profile will be disclosed with it. An independent editorial group is planned; its membership and disclosures will be published once it is established.

Appeals and correction

Companies will be invited to check factual descriptions and provide relevant information before publication. Published profiles will also provide a response and correction channel. Responses may include:

  • evidence the auditors did not have;
  • a request to reassess particular findings;
  • factual corrections.

Verified corrections will be dated and described in the update history. The company’s response will be published with the profile, including disagreements or additional evidence.

The channel for a company's response is stated on each published profile.

Review before publication

Draft profiles will be checked for source accuracy, the distinction between evidence and interpretation, and the treatment of uncertainty. Unverified concerns will remain clearly identified as open questions.

Profiles will describe the scope of the review and the evidence available at the time. They will support research and evaluation without providing product certification or regulatory approval.
